Fib_K14 is an injury-induced fibroblast state with epithelial-like plasticity at the epithelialization front
After defining the Epi-Front niche, we explored whether Fib_K14 represented an injury-associated fibroblast state transcriptionally related to epithelialization. 65 Proxy analysis positioned Fib_K14 near keratinocyte (KC) states, whereas SAC_SG_Progenitor provided a reference for classical SAC-associated epithelialization (Figure 3A). 66 Fib_K14 showed significantly higher KC proxy scores than other fibroblast subclusters (two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test, P < 0.05; Figure S3D). 67 PAGA further connected Fib_K14 with KC_Basal, KC_Basal_Mig and KC_Spinous_Mig states (Figure 3B and Figure S3E–S3F). 68 These computational relationships suggest transcriptional proximity but do not establish a lineage transition. 69 Quality-control, doublet-score and contamination-marker analyses showed no evidence that Fib_K14 resulted from disproportionate doublets or epithelial contamination (Figure S3A–S3C). Fib_K14 was rare in normal skin but increased after injury, differing significantly across healing periods (Kruskal–Wallis test, P < 0.05) and injury-depth groups (P < 0.01). Its abundance peaked at 15–28 dpb, corresponding to the active epithelialization period (Figure 3C-3D). Fib_K14 retained fibroblast-associated genes while expressing the epithelial-associated genes KRT14 and KRT5 (Figure 3E and Figure S3G). Its marker genes were enriched for extracellular matrix assembly, wound healing, keratinocyte differentiation and epidermis development (hypergeometric test, FDR-adjusted P < 0.05; Figure 3F and Figure S3H).
We next mapped Fib_K14 onto the x-y wound coordinate system. Fib_K14 was sparse and spatially dispersed at 5 dpb. Between 12 dpb and 26 dpb, its distribution shifted toward x values surrounding the Epi-Front and lower y values immediately beneath the advancing epithelium (Figure 3G). Additional wound sections reproduced this Epi-Front-associated distribution (Figure S3I). VIM/KRT5 immunofluorescence detected double-positive cells adjacent to the advancing epithelial tongue in 26-dpb DPTDI wounds (Figure 3H). Independent specimens showed corresponding VIM/KRT5 and VIM/KRT14 signals, whereas normal skin contained few double-positive cells (Mann–Whitney test, P < 0.05; Figure S3J and Figure S3K).
To determine whether these dual-feature cells were associated with a fibroblast lineage, we performed lineage tracing in Pdgfra-CreER;R26-EYFP mice. A subset of EYFP-labeled cells expressed KRT14 within wounded tissue, supporting the presence of injury-associated Pdgfra-lineage cells with epithelial-associated features (Figure 3I). This evidence supports lineage association without demonstrating complete fibroblast-to-keratinocyte conversion. PDGFRA-GFP+KRT5+ cells isolated by flow cytometry were more abundant in PTDI wounds than in normal skin (P < 0.05; Figure 3J and Figure S3M). 
To minimize keratinocyte (KC) carryover during flow cytometric isolation, we collected wounds from mice at 5 days after DPTDI. Only dermal tissue from the central wound bed was retained, whereas the overlying epidermis and surrounding wound-edge skin were excluded. This sampling strategy restricted the analyzed tissue to the intrawound dermal compartment and reduced the likelihood that epithelial-marker-positive events arose from contaminating KC. Flow cytometry subsequently identified a PDGFRA+EPCAM+ population within the wound-derived dermal cell suspension. Sequential gating of 10,000 events identified 619 events were PDGFRA+EPCAM+, corresponding to 6.19% of all analyzed events (Figure 3K). Viability, singlet and fluorescence controls further defined the sorted population and excluded major gating artifacts (Figure S3L). Together with the tissue-restricted sampling strategy, these results support the recovery of an intrawound dermal population co-expressing fibroblast- and epithelial-associated surface markers.
